# Delivery policy

Every editorial delivery contains two sequences: **Takes** and **Selects** (Assembly is an accepted alternate name for Selects). Takes contains all assessed good usable takes and attempts, grouped by independent recorded part and color coded when the current session supports that action. It is an editorial shortlist, not an exhaustive attempt archive. Failed, crew-only, uncertain, and unusable ranges remain internal analysis. Each delivery candidate requires an explicit usability status and reason; an on-topic usable partial attempt may be included. Selects contains one provisional or approved performance per part. Both start with all recorded hooks, followed by all body parts in editorial order.

An independent recorded part cannot disappear because its wording differs from the script or storyboard. On-topic performances belong to the intended part; keep the deviation visible in review notes. Distinguish the recorded-part inventory from overlapping script retrieval proposals: a phrase misclassified under several script IDs is one performance, and a crew voice is not an extra hook. Unrecorded script alternatives are reported as unrecorded, without manufacturing footage.

Use the existing head handles and add 3–5 frames to clip tails where adjacent speech and source bounds allow. Keep complete spoken words. Correct a selected restart to the clean restarted performance; Takes retains usable partial performances. Missing selections and uncertain outer cut boundaries block delivery instead of silently omitting parts. Uncertain boundaries or speaker identity remain internal analysis. Delivery never expands a usable take into a wide context containing neighboring failed attempts or crew speech. Retrieval aliases are accounted for once. Every recognized independent part must have a usable selected take; missing usability decisions stop planning instead of dropping a whole part.

Assess the recorder first, then compare every plausible dialogue channel for proximity: direct voice, low room tone and low reverb/echo. Loudness alone does not establish proximity. Keep similarly close main microphones when appropriate; mute tracks that fail the proximity criterion. Record the chosen media, stream/channel, evidence and uncertainty. Transcription uses only the main voice source, with crew speech excluded from the recorded-part inventory. Temporary ASR normalization does not alter delivered audio.

Implementation: `src/editorial/delivery.mjs` plans the pair from a revision-bound recorded-part inventory. The old shortlist and full-source organization outputs are internal analysis routes, not the delivery contract. Local acoustic measurements support a provisional microphone inference; they do not establish a listening judgment.

After creating or reusing an external transcript, import its timed words onto the exact original source media item as a native Premiere transcript before continuing the editorial workflow. Preserve the main-voice source clock; do not attach extracted-audio or timeline-relative timestamps to a different media item. Reuse matching native transcripts and reconcile conflicts without overwriting existing editor work. Premiere-created transcripts require verification rather than reimport. Sidecar files and SRT caption imports do not establish a native transcript. Save and read back the imported text and timings, then verify Text-panel visibility, save/reopen persistence and transcript coverage at the actual Takes/Selects sequence times, including any nesting. An import failure or uncertain result blocks progression for reconciliation. Caption creation and styling remain a separate finishing action; the prepared transcript lets an editor use Premiere's native Create captions from transcript later.

Before edits, check the current session's actual tools and permissions for foreground Premiere clip coloring, including a read-only UI check when a suitable computer-use tool exists. A bridge connection alone is insufficient. Claude Code may lack computer use, but decide from the current environment rather than the host or account name. Record `clipColoring: {available: true}` when usable, or `clipColoring: {available: false, reason: "<observed limitation>"}` when missing, denied or unconfirmed. In the unavailable case, skip coloring and its requested-color verification gates without pausing editing or asking for access, manual work or confirmation. Record `skipped_unavailable` and briefly note it in the final delivery; do not claim colors were applied or verified. Recheck in a new environment and use normal coloring whenever supported. An uncertain dispatched label action still requires reconciliation.

Construct Takes first, then finish its camera choices, main-voice routing and native part colors when available. Construct Selects by duplicating those prepared Takes and removing the alternative performances and any reviewed excess at the chosen take boundaries. Retain the existing script order and native instances; do not rebuild or recolor Selects from the original recording. The selected source intervals must be contained in usable Takes; missing containment stops planning for inventory reconciliation. When coloring is available, fresh saved-project evidence must establish every Takes part color before duplication and the requested Selects colors afterwards. Always verify inherited Selects colors, cameras, channels, effects and source clocks, including when part coloring was skipped. Replace inherited Takes markers with the Selects markers on the duplicate only. Preserve both Takes and the original source.

The delivery CLI is staged: `deliver config.json execute` creates Takes and reports preparation pending; after native preparation, `deliver config.json selects` derives Selects using the Takes ID recorded in the evidence file. An explicit `takesSequenceId` can identify an existing prepared Takes whose geometry matches the same inventory and source. The second stage verifies saved native inheritance, while proxy review exports and actual save/close/reopen acceptance remain separate requirements.

The delivery CLI accepts `voiceSources` for additional main-voice recordings, each with an exact source placement and revision-bound transcript. Its default boundary mode is `strict`; uncut uncertain contexts are restricted to internal analysis. Native instance colors, camera choices, audio routing, save/reopen state and actual exports require separate host verification.

Check proxy attachment and availability for the relevant media before footage-dependent editing or analysis. When proxies are already attached and available, use those proxies and ensure Premiere’s proxy toggle is ON; verify the setting. Existing attached proxies do not require automatic creation, replacement or reattachment, regardless of their resolution.

If proxies are not attached or their files are unavailable, offer assistant creation and attachment, attachment of existing proxies from a user-provided location, or manual creation/attachment by the user. Include an explicit option to use originals for this task. Wait for the user's decision before footage-dependent work; independent preparation may continue. A request to create proxies authorizes encoding, validation, attachment, enabling playback and saving without a repeated permission question; honor narrower files-only requests. Use the shared create-proxies skill for either encoder route. Default new 16:9 proxies to 1920×1080 QuickTime Apple ProRes 422 standard (10-bit 4:2:2), preserving source frame timing, duration, orientation, color, timecode and Premiere-recognized audio layout. Do not substitute ProRes 422 Proxy/LT/HQ, stretch other aspect ratios, upscale smaller media, or regenerate available attached proxies to enforce this default. Honor explicit user settings. Reconcile unavailable existing attachments before authorized reattachment or replacement. After attachment, verify that every relevant media item has an available matching proxy and that Premiere's proxy toggle is ON. Preserve original timeline geometry, source associations and audio channels. Use proxy-resolution review exports. Original-resolution exports require an explicit finishing request.

Preserve existing native audio/video clip links, including originally linked microphone channels and disabled audio. Record one original link-group snapshot in the duplication journal at the start of each Takes/Selects build. Complete the cuts, trims and reordering, then run one final pass over the finished sequence to restore the corresponding retained link groups and verify complete native membership. Keep performances in separate groups and leave originally unlinked clips unlinked. Individual edit operations do not capture or repair links. Unreadable original membership, ambiguous final targets, foreign groups or failed final restoration stop sequence completion for reconciliation. Verify links again when saving; a timed-out edit or relink must be read back without automatic replay.

Use one shared clip-color mapping per independent part in both Takes and Selects. Review status stays in markers and notes; it never replaces a part's clip color. When coloring is available, verify matching part colors from the actual saved native instances. When unavailable, keep the planned mapping and part markers, preserve existing colors and continue without making coloring a pending delivery requirement.

Trim excess silence at performance heads using the main voice audio. Wording acceptance and usability approval do not establish word timing. A first word with very low confidence, or a low-confidence timestamp stretched over an unusually long interval, requires a revision-bound audio-onset or editor boundary before planning. Keep a short frame-aligned head handle before the measured voice onset. Do not use a timestamp borrowed from the previous part as the next part's cut point.
