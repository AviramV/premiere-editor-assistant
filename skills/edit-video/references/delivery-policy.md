# Delivery policy

Every editorial delivery contains two sequences: **Takes** and **Selects** (Assembly is an accepted alternate name for Selects). Takes contains all assessed good usable takes and attempts, grouped by independent recorded part and color coded. It is an editorial shortlist, not an exhaustive attempt archive. Failed, crew-only, uncertain, and unusable ranges remain internal analysis. Each delivery candidate requires an explicit usability status and reason; an on-topic usable partial attempt may be included. Selects contains one provisional or approved performance per part. Both start with all recorded hooks, followed by all body parts in editorial order.

An independent recorded part cannot disappear because its wording differs from the script or storyboard. On-topic performances belong to the intended part; keep the deviation visible in review notes. Distinguish the recorded-part inventory from overlapping script retrieval proposals: a phrase misclassified under several script IDs is one performance, and a crew voice is not an extra hook. Unrecorded script alternatives are reported as unrecorded, without manufacturing footage.

Use the existing head handles and add 3–5 frames to clip tails where adjacent speech and source bounds allow. Keep complete spoken words. Correct a selected restart to the clean restarted performance; Takes retains usable partial performances. Missing selections and uncertain outer cut boundaries block delivery instead of silently omitting parts. Uncertain boundaries or speaker identity remain internal analysis. Delivery never expands a usable take into a wide context containing neighboring failed attempts or crew speech. Retrieval aliases are accounted for once. Every recognized independent part must have a usable selected take; missing usability decisions stop planning instead of dropping a whole part.

Assess the recorder first, then compare every plausible dialogue channel for proximity: direct voice, low room tone and low reverb/echo. Loudness alone does not establish proximity. Keep similarly close main microphones when appropriate; mute tracks that fail the proximity criterion. Record the chosen media, stream/channel, evidence and uncertainty. Transcription uses only the main voice source, with crew speech excluded from the recorded-part inventory. Temporary ASR normalization does not alter delivered audio.

Implementation: `src/editorial/delivery.mjs` plans the pair from a revision-bound recorded-part inventory. The old shortlist and full-source organization outputs are internal analysis routes, not the delivery contract. Local acoustic measurements support a provisional microphone inference; they do not establish a listening judgment.

The delivery CLI accepts `voiceSources` for additional main-voice recordings, each with an exact source placement and revision-bound transcript. Its default boundary mode is `strict`; uncut uncertain contexts are restricted to internal analysis. Native instance colors, camera choices, audio routing, save/reopen state and actual exports require separate host verification.

Check proxy attachment and availability for the relevant media before footage-dependent editing or analysis. When proxies are already attached and available, use those proxies and ensure Premiere’s proxy toggle is ON; verify the setting. Existing attached proxies do not require automatic creation, replacement or reattachment, regardless of their resolution.

If proxies are not attached or their files are unavailable, notify the user and ask whether they want to attach proxies first. Offer manual attachment by the user, or attachment by the assistant when the user explicitly requests it and provides the proxies location. Wait for the user’s decision before footage-dependent work; independent preparation may continue. Do not create, attach or replace proxies automatically. After attachment, verify that the relevant media has available proxies and that Premiere’s proxy toggle is ON. Preserve original timeline geometry, source associations and audio channels. Use proxy-resolution review exports. Original-resolution exports require an explicit finishing request.

Use one shared clip-color mapping per independent part in both Takes and Selects. Review status stays in markers and notes; it never replaces a part's clip color. Verify matching colors from the actual saved native instances.

Trim excess silence at performance heads using the main voice audio. Wording acceptance and usability approval do not establish word timing. A first word with very low confidence, or a low-confidence timestamp stretched over an unusually long interval, requires a revision-bound audio-onset or editor boundary before planning. Keep a short frame-aligned head handle before the measured voice onset. Do not use a timestamp borrowed from the previous part as the next part's cut point.
